Photo Tips

Simple photo tips that improve results fast

The tool can fix crop and background problems, but the source photo still matters. A better starting image gives you a more trustworthy final profile photo.

Use even lighting

Face a window or soft light source so your face looks clear and balanced. Avoid deep shadows across one side of the face.

Keep the camera at eye level

Eye-level photos look more confident and natural for job search and personal branding than extreme high or low angles.

Leave some space around your head

Do not crop too tightly in the original image. Extra room makes it easier to adjust framing for different size presets.

Wear clear, simple clothing

Solid colors usually work better than busy patterns. For professional use, visual clarity beats fashion complexity.

Use a neutral expression

A calm, approachable expression works across more contexts, from LinkedIn to client proposals to formal documents.

Start with the sharpest version you have

High-resolution photos produce cleaner edges and more believable background replacement than blurry screenshots.

Best Practices

Best practices for job seekers, freelancers, and official documents

A strong result is not only about aesthetics. It should also match the context where the photo will be used.

For LinkedIn, resumes, and personal branding

  • Default to a white background unless your brand system needs something else.
  • Keep your face centered and shoulders visible for a cleaner profile crop.
  • Reuse the same image across LinkedIn, portfolio, and freelance profiles for consistency.
  • Update the photo before major job-search pushes so all touchpoints feel current.

For passport, visa, and ID requirements

  • Always verify country-specific size, background color, and facial expression rules.
  • Do a final manual check before printing or submitting any official document photo.
  • Use the exact size preset required by the destination or application type.
  • When in doubt, prioritize compliance over style.

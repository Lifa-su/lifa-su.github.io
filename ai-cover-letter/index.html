Before you write

  • Write a letter when the role asks for one, when motivation matters, or when your move needs explanation.
  • Keep keywords natural and job-relevant so ATS can parse the fit without turning the letter into keyword soup.
  • Use only claims you can support with resume bullets, LinkedIn details, or examples you would repeat out loud later.

Safety checks

What weakens a cover letter fast

  • Invented enthusiasm that does not connect to a real team, mission, customer, or problem.
  • Claims with no proof, especially vague statements like "I am a perfect fit" or "I consistently exceed expectations."
  • New facts that do not appear anywhere in your resume, portfolio, or interview story.
  • Walls of text. Most roles only need a short note with one clear throughline.

Useful rules

Keep the letter in its lane

  • Use the letter to interpret evidence, not to create evidence.
  • One company-specific line is usually enough when the rest of the application already carries detail.
  • Anything you say here should also survive recruiter follow-up and interview questions.
